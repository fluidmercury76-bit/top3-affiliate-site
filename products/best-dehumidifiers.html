How to choose dehumidifiers

What does 20 litres per day really mean?

Meaco lists 20.45 L/day at 30°C and 80% relative humidity, but 8.50 L/day at 20°C and 60%, and 3.35 L/day at 10°C and 60%. Those are manufacturer figures for one machine, not measurements of your home or directly comparable figures for the other two models.

How do you estimate running cost?

Use input power in kW × hours running × your electricity rate. For an illustration, Meaco’s published 207 W at 20°C and 60% humidity would cost about 5.2p per hour at an assumed 25p/kWh if drawing that power continuously. This is an example tariff and operating condition, not a current price or a prediction of daily cost.

Laundry room or a cold spare room?

Check the operating temperature range and performance data before using any model in a cold space. For laundry, also think about room access, where the appliance can sit and how water will be emptied. Follow the clearance and drainage instructions; do not choose by the laundry-mode label alone.

Start with the source of moisture

A dehumidifier manages collected moisture; it does not repair a leaking pipe, roof or other building defect. Understand why the room is damp before treating a bigger extraction rating as the whole solution.

Match model codes, not just litres

Pro Breeze sells multiple dehumidifiers with a 20-litre headline. This comparison uses PB-D-08W-B-UK with a 5.5-litre tank and no Wi-Fi. Compare each offer’s full model code so app functions, drainage accessories and running figures are not borrowed from another product.

Frequently asked questions

Will a 20 L model collect 20 litres every day?

Not necessarily. Temperature and humidity change extraction substantially. Read the conditions attached to the published rating.

Does a bigger tank mean lower electricity use?

No. Tank capacity affects how much water can collect before emptying. Energy use depends on the appliance and operating conditions.

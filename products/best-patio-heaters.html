How to choose patio heaters

What does an hour of patio heating cost?

Use power in kW × your electricity rate. At an illustrative 25p/kWh, continuous use at 2 kW costs 50p per hour and 2.5 kW costs 62.5p. At the same example rate, 650 W costs about 16.3p per hour. These are arithmetic examples, not a current tariff or measured usage.

Wall-mounted or freestanding?

A wall-mounted unit keeps the floor clearer but ties the heater to one approved position. A stand needs floor space and a suitable cable route. Draw the table, chairs and walking routes before choosing, then check the exact installation instructions rather than assuming every location is acceptable.

Do not buy on coverage area alone

Exposure and seating layout affect how useful an outdoor heater will be. We have not measured heat distribution for these models. An IP rating does not remove the need to follow the manufacturer’s placement, weather and electrical guidance; confirm suitability for your particular patio.

Buying check 1

Infrared patio heaters warm people and surfaces in their line of sight. Placement, wind and distance matter more than a headline area-coverage claim. Follow the specific model's mounting heights and clearance instructions.

Buying check 2

Compare input power and the time you will actually use it. For illustration only, at 25p per kWh a 2 kW heater costs 50p per hour at full output, and a 2.5 kW heater costs 62.5p. Substitute your own electricity tariff.

Buying check 3

An outdoor IP rating is not permission to install a heater anywhere. Match the mounting method, electrical supply and clearances to its manual. Check whether brackets, a stand and remote control belong to the exact package being sold.

Is a 2,500 W heater cheaper to run than a 2,000 W one?

At full continuous power on the same tariff, 2,500 W uses more electricity per hour. Actual spending depends on settings and time in use.

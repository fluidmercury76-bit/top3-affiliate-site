How to choose air fryers

Start with your normal use, not the longest specification list. Compare usable basket capacity, footprint, single versus dual zones, cleaning, controls and whether you actually cook foods with different timings. The best choice is the model that removes the compromises you notice every week, not the one with the most features on paper.

Running cost and ownership

Electricity use is driven mainly by wattage and cooking time. A smaller chamber may finish sooner than a full oven, but the cheapest appliance to run is not automatically the cheapest to own. Prices, tariffs, consumables and stock change, so use current figures when you make the final comparison.

Common problems before you replace it

Uneven browning usually comes from overfilling or poor airflow; smoke often comes from fat or crumbs near the element; check drawer seating, clean the unit fully and follow the maker’s minimum-clearance guidance. If a product shows heat damage, swelling, exposed wiring or another safety fault, stop using it and follow the manufacturer’s support advice.

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose the best air fryer?

Start with how you will use it most often, then compare size, comfort, power or capacity, warranty, cleaning and the main trade offs. The best air fryer is the one that fits your normal use, not just the one with the longest feature list.

Is the premium air fryer always worth it?

No. Premium models are worth it when the extra performance, build quality or convenience will be used regularly. For occasional use, the best value pick is often the sharper buy.

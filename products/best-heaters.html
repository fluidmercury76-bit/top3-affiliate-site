How to choose portable heaters

Start with your normal use, not the longest specification list. Compare room size, thermostat control, heat delivery, noise, safety shut-offs, controls and whether the room is well insulated. The best choice is the model that removes the compromises you notice every week, not the one with the most features on paper.

Running cost and ownership

For resistance electric heaters, a kilowatt-hour of input becomes roughly a kilowatt-hour of heat at the point of use. The practical savings come from heating the right space for the right time with good thermostat control, not from claims of magically cheaper electric heat. Prices, tariffs, consumables and stock change, so use current figures when you make the final comparison.

Common problems before you replace it

If a heater cycles or stops, check thermostat setting, blocked airflow and overheat protection. Keep clearances around the unit, never cover it and avoid extension leads unless the manufacturer explicitly permits them. If a product shows heat damage, swelling, exposed wiring or another safety fault, stop using it and follow the manufacturer’s support advice.
